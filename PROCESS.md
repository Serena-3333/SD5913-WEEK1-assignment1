# Why Are We Here?- HOW I USED AI

## TOOLS and what I used them for

I used AI assistant (ChatGPT and TraeWork CN) through this essay for:

- **Brainstorming.** After watching the video and writing down key words and feelings about it, I pasted the assignment prompt into an AI and asked for angles worth exploring. I did not use any of the stock angles it offered — they were all too close to generic "AI won't replace artists" essays. Instead I gave the AI my own experience and my thoughts about how learning to code shaped my projects. The AI helped *surface* the thread that mattered to me.

- **Research / gathering sources.** I asked the AI to find references that would hold up in an academic essay, then verified every one before including it.

- **Structuring / outlining.** Once I had my own angle, I used the AI to turn it into a clean paragraph-level outline and to point out where two paragraphs were arguing the same point so I could merge them.

- **Translation.** My earliest outline was mixed Chinese–English, so I used AI for translation and for smoothing expression into natural English.

- **Editing.** I used AI to check grammar and tighten sentences.

## What I kept

Three things from the AI survived into the final essay. First, the **outline** it produced from my notes — the paragraph-by-paragraph skeleton I agreed with and then filled in. Second, **some of its points/arguments**, which I kept but revised where they did not match what I actually thought. Third, and most literally: this PROCESS.md itself was generated by the AI **from our conversation** — I asked it to look back over our chat and write up how the work had happened, and I kept that account because it was honest about both what I took and what I threw away.

## What I rejected

I rejected several generic paragraphs about AI replacing programmers. They were technically fluent but said nothing that was mine: phrases like "learning to code alongside AI makes you a more effective creator because you understand your tools" read well and are trivially true, which is precisely why I cut them — a claim that would survive any substitution is a claim without a position.

I also rejected an example the AI produced for me: a vivid but invented scene about grading a "warm dusk" into a game that felt "like a postcard, not an evening." It was better-written than anything I had, and that is exactly the danger. It was a synthetic memory, tidy and effective at pushing the reader toward agreement — but the essay is supposed to be built from my own practice, and a fabricated example would have collapsed the whole argument. I replaced it with the real incident described above. 

## What the AI was not used for 

I did not let the AI write the final prose. The essay's sentences and the haptics of the example are mine, drafted from my own notes and rewrite passes.
